Launcher look and feel

Three directions for the launcher (P14 in docs/PLAN.md), each on a phone held upright and on a PC screen. The flow, avatars, badges and stars are the same in all three, so you're comparing style alone.

Everything is sample: Pip and Bo, the games, the stars and the badges are made up. Tap around: players say hello and switch the home screens, games react, and grey badges show what earning one looks like.

A. Sticker book

Kraft paper, chunky outlines, tilted stickers. Badges stick onto the page like real stickers.

Feels like a scrapbook they're filling in. Middle effort: the paper, outlines and tilts are plain CSS, and sticking a badge on is one animation. The handwritten-style font is the thing to test with your 6-year-old while they're learning to read.

B. Bright playground

Bold flat colours, blobby shapes, buttons that squash and bounce. Each child's colour themes their home screen: tap Bo to see it change.

Feels like a toy. The simplest of the three to build well: the same tile grid rearranges for the phone and the PC, and new games just add a tile.

C. Adventure map

Games are islands and your avatar hops between them. Badges and recent games live in the treasure chest.

The most immersive and the most work: the phone and the PC each need their own map (compare the two), and every new game needs a spot on both. Badges are one tap further away, inside the chest.

Mockup only, not the launcher. Fonts from Google Fonts: Patrick Hand (A), Fredoka (B and C), Lilita One (C). Avatars are original animals drawn in code (P15); badges follow the starter set (P16); stars are the headline score (P17).
